Wu Lab @ Columbia · Medicine · Systems Biology

Decode and Target the Central Dogma

Our mission is to decode how the genome is written and read by leveraging RNA-centric functional genomics, CRISPR technologies, and machine learning. We translate these foundational discoveries into innovative therapies to prevent and treat heart disease, cancer, neurological disorders, and aging.

What we do

Research

Decode the noncoding genome

Uncover the design principles and molecular mechanisms of the 98% of the human genome that is noncoding.

  • Tiling mutagenesis of an entire gene/MYC (Shi, Yang, Tala et al., 2026 Cell)
  • How cells distinguish coding and noncoding information (Kesner et al., 2023 Nature)
  • Control mRNA 3′ ends by RNA structure (Wu & Bartel, 2017 Cell)
  • U1–PAS axis, promoter directionality, and new gene evolution (Wu & Sharp, 2013 Cell; Almada & Wu et al., 2013 Nature)

Ribosome & disease

Decipher the heart/muscle-specific RPL3L-containing ribosome in normal physiology and heart disease.

  • Pathogenic mechanisms and therapeutic development for RPL3L-linked dilated cardiomyopathy and heart failure (Murphy et al., 2026 Nature CVR)
  • Specialized function of the muscle-specific RPL3L-containing ribosome

CRISPR technology & therapy

Develop RNA-targeting CRISPR platforms and ASOs that modulate gene expression and reprogram cell fate in disease contexts.

  • RNA-guided cell targeting with CRISPR/Cas13 (Shi et al., 2023 Comm Biol; Shi et al., 2024 RNA Biol)
  • The first genome-wide analysis of Cas9 targeting in mammalian cells (Wu et al., 2014 Nature Biotechnology)
  • CRISPR/Cas technologies for editing the genome, epigenome, and transcriptome (Cong et al., 2013 Science; Ran et al., 2015 Nature; Liu et al., 2016 Cell; Liu et al., 2018 Cell; Zhang et al., 2018 Cell)

Computational biology & AI

Develop AI models for predicting RNA 3D structure and cell type/tissue-specific alternative splicing.

  • A web server called kpLogo for position-specific short motif analysis (Wu & Bartel, 2017 Nucleic Acids Research)
  • Network-based prediction of causal genes and pathways in human diseases (Wu et al., 2008 Molecular Systems Biology; Wu et al., 2009 Bioinformatics)
